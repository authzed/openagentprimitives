# Workshops and limits

<p className="doc-lede">
  A <strong>workshop</strong> is the isolated space one build runs in: its own namespace, its own permissions,
  its own sidecar identity, created when a builder session starts and torn down when the build is over. It is
  what lets the builder apply, test, and fix a draft freely without any of it touching the cluster's real
  agents.
</p>

## What a workshop holds

- **The draft** — the agent's definition, its connectors, its skills, applied inside the workshop and nowhere
  else. The builder's own tools can only reach objects in that namespace.
- **The test session** — when you press *Start the test*, the new agent runs there too, under the cluster's
  normal guards, with the workshop's identity.
- **Resource ceilings** — a maximum age, a cap on objects per kind, and a cap on test sessions, fixed at
  creation and not changeable from inside.

The builder's write tools are keyed on the draft, so the stage approval you give covers the draft in that
workshop and nothing else; a grant for one workshop means nothing in another.

## How many you may have open

Each person may keep a small number of workshops open at once — **three by default**, set cluster-wide by
`limits.maxWorkshopsPerStarter` in [settings](/docs/settings-config). "Open" means live: a build still going, or one
that finished but whose workshop has not been released yet.

Starting a builder session past the limit is refused before anything is created. The refusal names the open
workshops by their session names and states, and says what to do:

> You already have the maximum number of builder workshops open at once. Finish or close one before starting
> another. Open now: agent-builder-8659e605 (in progress), agent-builder-c179e6f3 (in progress),
> agent-builder-d41b45db (finished). Open one of them and ask it to close the others.

## Closing workshops

Open any of your builder sessions and ask it, in the conversation or on the page, to close your other
workshops. It closes all of them, or only the ones you name — by the session name the refusal showed you —
and tells you what was closed, what was refused and why, and anything still being decided. It never closes
the workshop it is in.

Closing a workshop ends that builder session and deletes the workshop; its saved draft, if it reached
delivery, is an attachment in that conversation and outlives the workshop.

A platform admin can name anyone's workshop; anyone else can name only their own. If you ask a second time
while the first ask is still being decided, the builder waits for that answer rather than asking again, and
says so — a workshop opened since then is not covered until you ask again.

## What releases a workshop

You do not have to close a finished build. A workshop is released on its own once its builder session has
ended **and** any install request it made has been decided — installed, declined, or failed. Hand-off has no
request to wait for, so a session that ends after *Hand the draft to someone* releases immediately. Released
workshops stop counting toward the limit.

<Callout kind="note" title="For admins">
  The [admin console](/docs/admin-dashboard) lists every open workshop with its starter, its install request if it
  made one, and the option to delete it outright. The per-person limit is `spec.limits.maxWorkshopsPerStarter`
  on the cluster's settings; the list of classes allowed to open a workshop at all is
  `spec.limits.builderClasses`, seeded by <code>oap install</code> for the builder and never widened by a
  namespace's own settings.
</Callout>
