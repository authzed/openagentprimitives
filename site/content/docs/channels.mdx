# Channels & continuity

<div className="doc-lede">
  An agent is only useful if a human can reach it, follow what it's doing, and step in. The usual failures: the
  agent lives where nobody is (a separate web app instead of Slack or the ticket queue), there's no real thread
  to follow (only a transcript the framework owns), and anyone who can type can drive it. OAP reaches agents
  <strong>where teams already work</strong>, with durable threads and approvals that live in-channel.
</div>

<Clip name="channels-demo" />

## How it works

A **channel** is a pluggable transport: Slack, a browser tab, a terminal, a webhook. The same agent is reachable
through any of them, and the conversation is a real, durable thread — it continues across turns, and everyone in
it sees the same plan, the same status, and the same approvals. In Slack you can even **@-mention the bot into a
thread that's already going**, bringing the prior discussion and the people in it into the session. Crucially, the approval cards a human acts on
render *in the channel*, next to the work, rather than in some separate console — and who is allowed to drive or
approve is an authorization question, not "whoever can type."
