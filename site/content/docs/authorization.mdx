# Authorization

<p className="doc-lede">
  An agent that calls tools acts on systems that have owners, so something has to answer one question for every
  action: <strong>may this subject do this, to this resource, right now?</strong> Most systems collapse that
  into authentication (holding a token means you're allowed), scatter checks across ad-hoc <code>if</code>
  statements, and have no resource-level granularity. OAP checks every action against a <strong>SpiceDB
  graph</strong>.
</p>

## Three gates, one graph

Authorization in OAP is one relationship graph consulted three ways:

- **Every action is checked.** Each tool call resolves to a permission on a resource and is checked
  per-action — not once at the start, but every time.
- **The risky ones ask a human.** Actions whose effects leave the session, or that reach an owned resource, pause
  for an explicit human approval — and the person who may approve is derived from the graph, not from whoever
  happens to be talking.
- **Access is earned, then scoped.** An approval doesn't hand over a standing key; it writes a grant scoped to
  exactly what was approved — this plan, this resource, this session — and no more.

Because the unit of trust is the *resource*, "who can decide this" is answered by who owns the resource, and an
approval can never be transplanted onto something it wasn't for.

## Go deeper

- **[Multiplayer sessions](#/multiplayer-sessions)** — more than one human in a shared session, gated at the
  door and at the dangerous step.
- **[Plan gating & approvals](#/plan-gating)** — approving a whole multi-step plan at once, scoped to exactly
  the plan — and the exact resources — it named.
- **[Shared permissions](#/shared-permissions)** — data owned by a resource, released only with its owner's
  approval, routed to the owner rather than the requester.
- **[Information leakage & egress](#/information-leakage)** — the right data reaching the wrong audience is a
  permission question too, gated per-datum and routed to the data owner.
